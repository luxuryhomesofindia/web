When buying land in Tamil Nadu, Patta records revenue landholding details, Chitta provides land classification particulars, and an Encumbrance Certificate (EC) tracks registered sales and mortgages via TNREGINET. Tamil Nadu's March 2026 Integrated Land Record initiative now combines textual Patta/Chitta data with FMB maps online via e-Services and Tamil Nilam. Always cross-check survey numbers, extent, and 30-year parent deeds with an independent property lawyer before paying an advance.

When buying a plot in Chennai or elsewhere in Tamil Nadu, Patta, Chitta and the Encumbrance Certificate (EC) help you examine different aspects of the property's records. Patta relates to recorded landholding information, Chitta is a historical revenue-record term associated with land particulars, and an EC helps identify registered transactions for a property and search period. These records serve different purposes and should be read together with the title deeds and other relevant documents.

For anyone planning to build a custom home, the distinction matters: checking revenue records is not the same as establishing legal title, and establishing title is not the same as confirming that a proposed house is permitted. A careful review should reconcile property identifiers, ownership documents, transaction history, survey particulars and applicable planning approvals. No single record guarantees that a plot is legally safe to buy or suitable for the intended development.

2. What is Patta?

Patta is a revenue record associated with a landholding and the person or persons recorded against it. Depending on the applicable record, it may include the recorded holder, survey number, subdivision and extent.

Cross-checking recorded holder name, survey number, and extent between a Patta document and registered Sale Deed.

What should you check in Patta?

  • Recorded name: Does it correspond with the seller's claimed ownership?
  • Survey number and subdivision: Do the identifiers match the title deed?
  • Extent: Is the recorded extent consistent with the documents available?
  • Transfer history: Are recent changes supported by relevant revenue mutation orders?

A name mismatch does not automatically invalidate a transaction, but it must be explained using supporting documents rather than an unsupported assurance from the seller.

3. What is Chitta? (Integrated 2026 Records)

Chitta is a historical Tamil Nadu revenue-record term associated with land particulars, including classification information. With digitisation, buyers encounter combined or integrated records rather than separate paper documents.

Integrated Land Record combining textual Record of Rights, Field Measurement Book (FMB) sketches, and survey plot dimensions.

2026 Integrated Land Record & Patta Transfer History Updates

Tamil Nadu's Integrated Land Record service (launched March 2026) combines Record of Rights, FMB sketches, and Chitta data into a single digital output.

Access the official e-Services portal or the Tamil Nilam Citizen Portal to verify Patta Transfer History for private lands from 2016 onward.

4. What is an Encumbrance Certificate (EC)?

An Encumbrance Certificate helps identify registered transactions appearing in the relevant registration records for a specified property and search period. It is an essential component of investigating a property's transaction history.

Official TNREGINET Registration Portal

Search and download ECs directly via the official TNREGINET portal. Always search for at least 30 to 40 years to cover parent deeds.

Does a nil EC mean the property is safe to buy?

No. A nil EC does not establish that every possible legal claim or title defect is absent. Its meaning depends on property identifiers, search period, and registration records covered. Unregistered family partition claims or boundary disputes will not appear on an EC.

5. Which document should you check first? (5-Step Workflow)

  1. Step 1: Identify the exact property: Start with the title deed and note survey numbers, subdivisions, extents, and boundaries.
  2. Step 2: Review applicable revenue records: Check Patta/Chitta and A-Register extracts on the official e-Services portal.
  3. Step 3: Examine the EC: Run a 30–40 year EC search on TNREGINET for the exact survey numbers.
  4. Step 4: Reconcile information: Cross-verify names, survey numbers, and extents across deeds, revenue extracts, and EC entries.
  5. Step 5: Obtain independent legal review: Engage a qualified Tamil Nadu property lawyer and a licensed surveyor.

6. How to check Patta and Chitta-related records online

Visit the Tamil Nadu e-Services Portal:

  • Select "View Patta / Chitta".
  • Choose District, Taluk, and Village.
  • Select search by Patta Number or Survey / Sub-division Number.
  • Retrieve the digital record and verify the QR code and digital signature.

7. How to check an EC online in Tamil Nadu (TNREGINET)

Visit TNREGINET:

  • Select "Encumbrance Certificate" -> "Search EC".
  • Enter Zone, District, Sub-Registrar Office (SRO), Village, and Survey Details.
  • Set the search start and end dates (minimum 30 years recommended).
  • Download the search output and review each registered document number against parent deeds.

8. What if Patta, Chitta and EC do not match?

Discrepancy Scenario Appropriate Next Step
Seller's name differs from Patta Trace acquisition history and request Patta mutation order records
Survey number or sub-division differs Compare deed schedules, FMB survey sketches, and revenue extracts
Plot extent differs Engage a licensed surveyor to measure physical site vs deed schedules
EC shows an open mortgage entry Demand a registered Mortgage Discharge / Release Deed from the bank
Expected sale deed missing from EC Recheck SRO jurisdiction, survey sub-divisions, and search years
Land classification is agricultural or waterbody Verify land-use conversion & CMDA/DTCP development restrictions

9. Are Patta, Chitta and EC enough to buy a plot safely?

No. Revenue records and ECs are critical, but they answer only part of the due diligence equation.

Before buying a plot for a custom or luxury home, also verify:

  • 30+ years of registered parent deeds.
  • Seller's legal right and capacity (Power of Attorney validity, legal heir certificates).
  • Lawful physical road access to the plot.
  • Independent CMDA / DTCP layout planning approvals.
  • Coastal Regulation Zone (CRZ) or FSI development constraints.

Final takeaway

Patta, Chitta-related records and EC are complementary parts of property due diligence, not competing documents. Revenue records help examine recorded landholding information, while an EC helps investigate registered transactions. Neither independently guarantees clear title or confirms that the plot can be developed as intended.

Before purchasing land in Chennai or elsewhere in Tamil Nadu, reconcile the available records with the title deeds, survey particulars and applicable planning records. Resolve unexplained differences with appropriate legal and technical assistance before committing to the purchase.
