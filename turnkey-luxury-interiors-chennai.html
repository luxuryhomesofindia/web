What does a turnkey interior project include?

A turnkey interior project is a delivery arrangement covering the stages agreed between the homeowner and the service provider. Depending on the contract, these may include design development, specifications, procurement, fabrication, installation and completion.

The term alone does not establish that every item is included.

Before appointing an interior team, clarify:

  • Design & Drawings: Which design services, 3D visualisations, and 2D working drawings are included.
  • Material Specifications: Who prepares and approves detailed material, veneer, stone, and hardware specifications.
  • Procurement Scope: Which materials, surface tiles, sanitary fittings, and appliances will be supplied.
  • Bespoke Joinery: Whether custom furniture, wardrobes, wall paneling, and cabinetry are included.
  • Service Coordination: Who coordinates electrical, plumbing, HVAC, and civil interface works.
  • Specialist Subcontractors: Which specialist vendors (automation, acoustic ceiling, home cinema) are involved.
  • Handover & Snagging: What installation quality checks, deep cleaning, and snagging rectification cover.
  • Contract Exclusions: Which costs, statutory fees, exclusions, and homeowner responsibilities remain separate.

A clear written scope helps you compare proposals on what will actually be delivered, rather than on the label attached to the package.

Begin with the way you want to live

A considered interior should reflect your routines, preferences and priorities.

You may need more practical storage, comfortable spaces for entertaining, a kitchen suited to family life or bedrooms designed around rest and privacy. These requirements should inform the layout, furniture and material decisions from the beginning.

Discuss the following during your initial brief:

  • Room Usage: How each room will be used during daily routines and entertaining.
  • Circulation Clearances: Furniture dimensions, doorway clearances, and comfortable walking paths.
  • Storage & Accessibility: Customized storage solutions for wardrobes, kitchen pantries, and linen.
  • Finishes & Materials: Preferred natural woods (teak, walnut), marble slabs, brass accents, and textures.
  • Existing Furniture: Family heirlooms or artwork to retain and integrate into new designs.
  • Layered Lighting: Task, ambient, and accent lighting tailored to room activities.
  • Smart-Home Features: Desired automation levels for lighting scenes, shades, and climate.
  • Maintenance Expectations: Long-term durability and maintenance needs for coastal Chennai weather.

The aim is a coherent home that feels personal and functions well—not a collection of unrelated design upgrades.

Define the scope room by room

South Indian contemporary dining and kitchen interior featuring custom solid wood dining set, natural stone island, and warm LED cove lighting.

Living and dining areas

Coordinate seating, dining furniture, lighting, circulation and storage. Review how the rooms work during everyday use and when guests visit. If media units, display joinery or bespoke furniture are required, identify them in the scope and confirm their dimensions before fabrication.

Bedrooms and wardrobes

Bring wardrobes, beds, bedside furniture, lighting and finishes into a coordinated plan. Storage should respond to the occupant's needs, with practical internal arrangements, comfortable access and clearances for doors and drawers.

Master bedroom suite in Chennai featuring full-height teak wardrobe joinery, woven reed accents, and peaceful courtyard garden views.

Kitchens and utility spaces

Coordinate cabinetry, countertops, appliances, plumbing, ventilation and electrical connections. Confirm which appliances are supplied by the contractor, homeowner or another vendor. Final dimensions and service connections should be reviewed before cabinetry is fabricated.

Bathrooms

Review fittings, surfaces, lighting, waterproofing and service interfaces. Clarify whether plumbing alterations, waterproofing, fixtures and associated civil work are included or require separate arrangements.

Custom furniture

Define dimensions, materials, upholstery, finishes and installation requirements. Bespoke pieces should suit the room, intended use and maintenance needs rather than simply reproduce a reference image.

Smart-home integration

Plan automation alongside the electrical layout and interior design. Confirm who specifies, supplies, installs, configures and supports each system. Check device compatibility, control locations, wiring requirements and access for maintenance.

Know what is included—and what is not

A well-defined interior proposal should separate included work from optional items and homeowner-supplied products.

Confirm the treatment of:

  • Design drawings and visualisations.
  • Material selection and specifications.
  • Joinery fabrication and site installation.
  • Loose furniture and furnishings.
  • Appliances and decorative lighting.
  • Curtains and soft furnishings.
  • Electrical and plumbing modifications.
  • Automation equipment and configuration.
  • Site preparation and protection.
  • Installation checks and completion documentation.

Do not assume that “complete interiors” means every item needed to occupy the home is covered. Ask for a written inclusions and exclusions schedule before approving the appointment.

Build a budget around specifications

Interior costs depend on the rooms covered, quantities, selected materials, product specifications and work included. A package total is difficult to evaluate when the underlying assumptions are unclear.

A useful proposal should identify:

  • Product or material specifications (plywood grade, timber type, stone brand).
  • Quantities and allowances for undecided selections.
  • Fabrication and installation responsibilities.
  • Procurement arrangements.
  • Fees, applicable taxes and exclusions.
  • Payment milestones linked to verified progress.
  • Variation and approval procedures.

Keep decisions and approvals documented

Coordinated delivery still requires timely homeowner decisions.

Layouts, samples, finishes and equipment may need approval before procurement or fabrication begins. Changes after an order is placed or work is manufactured can affect cost, availability and the programme.

From brief to completion: Suggested project framework

  1. Establish the Requirements: Share available floor plans, site photographs, room requirements, reference images and budget priorities.
  2. Develop the Design Direction: Review layouts, materials, lighting, furniture and continuity between spaces.
  3. Confirm the Scope: Document specifications, allowances, responsibilities, exclusions and approval procedures.
  4. Coordinate Supply & Installation: Agree on the proposed programme, procurement responsibilities and interfaces with other work.
  5. Review Completion: Define installation checks, snagging, outstanding items and relevant product documentation in the agreement.
