Executive Summary: Auditing Per-Square-Foot Quotations

  • Rate vs. Total Scope: A per-square-foot construction rate helps initiate financial planning, but the number alone does not determine the total cost of your finished home.
  • Area Measurement Rules: Plot area, carpet area, built-up area, and chargeable area are distinct terms. Confirm how walls, staircases, balconies, covered parking, and voids are calculated in writing.
  • Material Allowances: A material allowance sets a selection budget; it does not mean every premium finish is included. Always audit unit price allowances for flooring, sanitaryware, and joinery.
  • Exclusions & Site Logistics: Foundation engineering for high water tables, municipal sanction fees, external compound walling, landscaping, and DG backup systems often sit outside headline civil rates.

A per-square-foot rate can help you begin planning a luxury home in Chennai. But the number alone does not tell you what your finished home will cost.

The total depends on the area used for billing, the construction scope, the selected materials, site requirements and the work included in the agreement. Two quotations with different rates may cover different things.

Which area is used to calculate the construction price?

The area used for billing is one of the first details to clarify. Plot area, carpet area, built-up area and chargeable area are not interchangeable terms.

Area Term What It Generally Describes
Plot area Land within the property boundary.
Carpet area Usable internal floor area under the applicable definition.
Built-up area Floor area including additional building components, depending on the measurement convention.
Chargeable area The area defined in the construction quotation for calculating the price.

Do not assume every contractor uses the same measurement method. Ask the contractor to state how the quotation treats walls, staircases, balconies, covered parking, service spaces, terraces, double-height areas and external structures.

Your quotation should identify the chargeable area and explain how it was calculated.

How do you calculate a per-square-foot construction estimate?

The basic calculation is:

Agreed Chargeable Area × Applicable Rate Per Sq Ft = Package-Based Construction Amount

For example, a calculation is meaningful only when both the chargeable area and the applicable rate have been agreed. The resulting amount may still exclude work that is separately priced or outside the package.

Before using the calculation for budgeting, confirm:

  1. The measurement method and chargeable area.
  2. The rate and its validity period.
  3. The specifications covered by that rate.
  4. The exclusions, allowances and taxes.
  5. How additional work and changes are approved.

Do not use plot area as a substitute for chargeable building area unless the written quotation expressly defines it that way.

What is a material allowance?

A material allowance sets out the budget or selection basis for a particular item. It does not automatically mean that every product in that category is included.

For flooring, bathroom fittings and other finishes, clarify:

  • The allowance amount and the unit it applies to.
  • The quantity covered.
  • Whether installation is included.
  • How an upgrade is priced.
  • Whether changing a product affects associated work.
  • Who approves the selection and when it must be finalized.

Product substitutions may affect procurement and installation as well as purchase cost. Confirm the impact before approving a change.

Why can construction rates differ between homes?

Site and foundation requirements

Ground conditions, excavation, access and the foundation design required for the site can change the construction scope. Confirm the relevant investigation and engineering requirements rather than assuming a standard foundation.

Building design and configuration

Floor count, room layouts, large openings, staircases, double-height spaces and other architectural features may affect quantities and execution.

Materials and finishes

Different specifications can produce different quotations. Compare the actual product descriptions and allowances rather than the labels used to describe them.

Building services and specialist features

Cooling equipment, hot-water systems, home automation, pools and private lifts may be included, excluded or separately priced depending on the agreement.

Exclusions and variations

A lower headline rate may leave more work outside the package. Compare the likely total scope, not just the first number shown.

For a Chennai property, discuss the actual site's ground conditions, drainage, water exposure, access and service requirements with the relevant professionals. Do not assume that all sites have identical requirements.

What costs may sit outside the main construction rate?

Confirm each item against the written proposal instead of assuming it is included. Potential exclusions may include:

  • Land acquisition and survey fees.
  • Architectural and structural consultant fees.
  • Statutory CMDA/GCC sanction charges and approvals.
  • Interior work beyond the stated scope.
  • Specialist equipment and amenities (lifts, swimming pools).
  • Compound walls, driveways, and external development.
  • Taxes (GST), where applicable.
  • Approved design changes and additional work variations.

Note on LHI Package Exclusions: Structural foundation dewatering for coastal ECR sites, private elevators, swimming pools, architectural design fees, and municipal sanction charges are typically itemized separately from basic shell civil rates to ensure complete line-item transparency.

How should you compare two construction quotations?

Use the same checklist for each builder:

Comparison Point Question to Ask Builder
Chargeable area Are both quotations based on the same measurement convention?
Structure Are the site assumptions and structural scope comparable?
Materials Are equivalent specifications and brands or quality levels identified?
Allowances Are quantities, budgets and upgrade rules documented?
Services Are electrical, plumbing and equipment inclusions comparable?
Interiors Are kitchens, wardrobes, furniture and lighting included?
Exclusions Are taxes, external works, approvals and specialist items accounted for?
Variations Is there a written process for pricing and approving changes?
Handover Are the deliverables, testing and handover requirements clear?

A lower rate does not automatically mean a lower complete project cost. A fair comparison requires comparable specifications and a documented list of everything that remains to be paid for.
