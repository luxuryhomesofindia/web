Red Flags When Hiring a Builder: 15 Warning Signs Every Homeowner Should Know

Published: October 16, 2026 Luxury Homes of India Editorial 14 Min Read Chennai & Tamil Nadu
Introduction Lead

Choosing a builder is one of the most important decisions you make when planning a home. The wrong choice can expose you to unclear costs, inconsistent workmanship, delays and disputes. The most important red flags when hiring a builder include an unusually low quotation without a clear scope, refusal to provide a detailed bill of quantities (BOQ), pressure for a large advance, vague material specifications, missing contractual safeguards and reluctance to provide credible project references. Homeowners should also examine site supervision, quality-control procedures, approval requirements, change orders and post-handover support. Before committing money, compare quotations against the same drawings and specifications, verify relevant project experience, and document payment milestones, completion requirements and defect responsibilities. For a house in Chennai, check the applicable planning-permission route for the property rather than relying on verbal assurances. No single warning sign proves misconduct, but repeated evasiveness about essential documentation deserves serious attention.

Section: Why checking matters

Why checking a builder matters before you sign

A builder's quotation, portfolio and verbal promises tell only part of the story. You also need to understand what the contract covers, who supervises the work, how technical decisions are made and what happens when the scope or schedule changes.

For homeowners planning residential construction in Chennai, the aim is not simply to find the lowest price. It is to select a provider whose proposed scope, documentation, technical responsibilities and communication arrangements can be evaluated before the project begins.

Use the 15 warning signs below as a practical screening guide. Assess each concern in context, request written clarification and seek independent professional advice when a technical or contractual issue cannot be resolved.

Red Flag 1

1. An unusually low quotation with no clear explanation

A lower quotation is not automatically a problem. It becomes a concern when the builder cannot explain how the price was calculated or why essential items are missing.

One proposal might include waterproofing, electrical installations, plumbing fittings and painting, while another covers only part of that work. The headline prices are not directly comparable.

What to check:

  • Ask for a detailed BOQ or equivalent itemised scope.
  • Compare material specifications and allowances.
  • Identify exclusions, provisional amounts and additional charges.
  • Confirm whether external works and interior fittings are included.
Homeowner action: Compare every quotation against the same drawings, specifications and scope before choosing a builder.
Red Flag 2

2. Refusal to provide a detailed BOQ

A per-square-foot rate may be a convenient starting point, but it does not establish everything that will be delivered.

A sufficiently detailed BOQ or scope document should make it easier to understand the work items, quantities where applicable, rates, specifications and responsibilities.

Ask for clarification on:

  • Civil and structural work
  • Cement, steel and masonry specifications
  • Flooring, doors and windows
  • Electrical and plumbing installations
  • Waterproofing, painting and finishing
  • Labour, materials, exclusions and allowances
Core Warning: The warning sign is not the use of per-square-foot pricing itself. It is refusing to explain what the quoted price actually includes.
Red Flag 3

3. Pressure to pay a large advance before the scope is defined

An advance may be linked to agreed mobilisation or material procurement. The risk arises when the amount has no clear purpose, supporting documentation or contractual protection.

Before paying, ask how the amount will be used, how it will be adjusted against future payments and what happens if the project does not proceed.

Protect yourself by:

  • Agreeing on the payment purpose in writing.
  • Recording the adjustment or refund mechanism.
  • Identifying the documents required to support procurement.
  • Obtaining receipts and retaining payment records.
  • Checking the contract's termination provisions.

Do not rely on a universal advance-payment percentage. The appropriate arrangement depends on the contract and the safeguards in place.

Red Flag 4

4. No proper written construction agreement

A quotation or verbal understanding may not fully define the responsibilities of the homeowner and builder.

Before signing, ensure the agreement addresses:

  • Scope, approved drawings and specifications
  • Contract value, taxes where applicable and payment milestones
  • Completion schedule and delay provisions
  • Variation approval and cost adjustments
  • Quality requirements, warranties and defect rectification
  • Termination and dispute resolution

For a substantial project, consider an independent legal review before committing.

Red Flag 5

5. Material brands and specifications remain verbal

Terms such as “premium quality,” “best materials” and “branded products” are too vague to define the complete technical requirement.

The contract should identify applicable material grades, product specifications or approved alternatives. It should also explain whether substitutions require the homeowner's written approval.

Ask how purchase records, relevant test results and technical approvals will be documented.

A material should not be accepted solely because its brand is familiar. Its suitability must also match the project's specifications and the responsible professional's recommendations.

Red Flag 6

6. The builder proposes starting before required approvals are in place

Do not assume that an application, an agent's assurance or a promise to regularise the work later is equivalent to an issued approval.

Before construction, confirm which permissions and conditions apply to the property, who is responsible for the application and what documentation has been issued.

For projects in Chennai or elsewhere in Tamil Nadu, begin with the relevant authority and the official Tamil Nadu planning-permission portal. Confirm that the selected route applies to the specific property and proposal.

Approval requirements can differ by location, property characteristics and the nature of the work.

Red Flag 7

7. Soil investigation and structural engineering are dismissed

A neighbouring house's foundation is not proof that the same design is appropriate for your property.

Foundation decisions depend on the site's conditions, the proposed structure and the engineering assessment. The need and scope of soil investigation should be determined by the qualified professionals responsible for the project.

Ask who is responsible for structural design, what site information is required, how reinforcement and formwork are inspected, and which testing and technical records will be maintained.

Red Flag 8

8. The builder cannot provide credible project references

Photographs and brochures can demonstrate a design style, but they do not independently establish workmanship, reliability or after-handover support.

Where possible, request permission-based visits to completed projects and speak directly with previous clients.

Ask about cost changes, communication, delays, finishing quality, defect rectification and the builder's response after occupation.

Privacy and access restrictions can be legitimate. The concern is a persistent inability or refusal to provide any credible, independently verifiable evidence of relevant experience.

Red Flag 9

9. No clearly identified site supervisor

Construction involves multiple trades, deliveries, inspections and decisions. Someone needs clear responsibility for daily coordination.

Before signing, establish:

  • Who is responsible for site supervision?
  • How often will that person attend the site?
  • Who coordinates subcontractors and suppliers?
  • How are progress and quality checks recorded?
  • Who can issue site instructions or approve changes?

Meet the person responsible for your project where possible. Clarify the reporting and escalation process so technical questions do not depend on informal conversations alone.

Red Flag 10

10. Reasonable questions about quality are dismissed

The finished appearance of a house does not reveal every aspect of its construction quality.

Ask how the project will address reinforcement inspections, concrete requirements, waterproofing, electrical and plumbing testing, concealed installations and defect resolution.

The inspection and testing plan should reflect the approved drawings, project specifications and requirements set by the responsible professionals.

A useful question to ask:
“What is checked, who checks it, when is it checked, and where is the record kept?”
An answer that identifies the process and responsible person is far more useful than a general promise of excellent workmanship.
Red Flag 11

11. Additional costs appear without written justification

Costs can legitimately change when the homeowner requests a modification, actual quantities vary or a contractual adjustment applies.

The warning sign is an unexplained increase, an unsupported charge or work undertaken without the approval required by the agreement.

Every proposed variation should identify the change, reason, additional or reduced cost, schedule impact and revised specifications where applicable.

Keep a record of the original contract value, approved variations, payments and remaining balance.

Red Flag 12

12. The completion date is an unsupported promise

A realistic construction schedule should account for approvals, procurement, labour coordination, inspections, curing requirements, finishing choices and owner decisions.

Ask for a stage-wise programme with major milestones, dependencies and a process for communicating delays.

The contract should explain how extensions are handled and what happens if a contractual completion requirement is not met.

A schedule that can be reviewed and updated is more useful than a confident promise of unusually rapid completion.

Red Flag 13

13. Communication is unreliable before the contract is signed

Repeatedly unanswered questions, inconsistent explanations and reluctance to clarify costs may indicate that project communication needs improvement.

Agree on a primary contact, the frequency of progress updates, how instructions will be recorded and how urgent issues will be escalated.

A clear communication system should distinguish routine updates from decisions that require formal written approval.

The objective is not instant replies at all hours. It is a reliable process for resolving decisions that affect cost, quality or schedule.

Red Flag 14

14. Warranty and defect responsibilities are vague

Statements such as “we will fix everything” do not define what is covered or how a defect will be handled.

Ask for written terms covering the applicable warranty period, coverage, exclusions, reporting process, rectification responsibilities and maintenance obligations.

Different components and types of work may be subject to different terms. Check the contract and applicable legal requirements rather than assuming one warranty period covers every issue.

Red Flag 15

15. Support disappears after handover

The handover should establish what has been completed, which documents have been delivered and how outstanding defects will be recorded.

Before final settlement, check the agreed handover requirements, available product and system documentation, applicable warranties and the process for resolving the outstanding defect list.

Record unresolved items and obtain written acknowledgement in accordance with the contract.

A clear handover process helps the homeowner understand the status of the work and the next steps for any agreed rectification.

Section: RERA

Does RERA registration apply to every builder?

No. Do not assume that every contractor engaged to construct a private house is subject to the same RERA registration requirements as a promoter marketing a real estate project.

Applicability depends on the arrangement and the relevant statutory requirements. Where a real estate project is being developed or marketed, check the applicable rules and the official Tamil Nadu Real Estate Regulatory Authority website. Obtain legal clarification if the arrangement is unclear.

Section: What should you do

What should you do if a builder raises concerns?

  • Pause before signing or making a substantial payment.
  • Identify the specific missing document, unclear charge or technical question.
  • Request written clarification and retain the response.
  • Verify relevant claims with previous clients, professionals or authorities.
  • Compare alternative proposals against the same scope.
  • Seek independent legal or technical advice for material concerns.
  • Consider another provider if essential safeguards continue to be refused.
Key Takeaway: A single unresolved question does not establish dishonesty. A repeated pattern of evasiveness about essential safeguards, however, is a sound reason to reconsider the engagement.
Section: Homeowner's Checklist

Homeowner's checklist before hiring a builder

  • I have compared quotations against the same scope.
  • I understand the BOQ, specifications and exclusions.
  • Material substitutions require a defined approval process.
  • I have checked relevant project references where available.
  • The responsible technical professionals and site supervisor are identified where applicable.
  • The construction agreement defines payment milestones and variations.
  • The schedule and delay provisions are documented.
  • Applicable approvals and technical assessments have been addressed.
  • Warranty, defect rectification and handover terms are written.
  • The purpose and adjustment of the advance are clear.
  • Significant unresolved legal or technical concerns have been independently reviewed.

If essential safeguards remain unresolved, postpone the commitment until you understand the risk and the necessary documentation is in place.

FAQ Section

Frequently Asked Questions

What are the biggest red flags when hiring a builder?
The main warning signs include an unexplained low quotation, refusal to define the scope, pressure for a large advance, vague material specifications, missing contractual safeguards and inability to provide credible project references.
Is a low construction quotation always a warning sign?
No. A competitive price can be legitimate. Ask the builder to explain the difference and compare inclusions, exclusions, specifications and allowances before deciding.
Should I pay an advance before signing a construction agreement?
Avoid a substantial commitment without written terms that explain the advance's purpose, adjustment or refund arrangements and what happens if the project does not proceed.
How can I check whether a builder is reliable?
Verify relevant business details, examine completed work where possible, contact previous clients with permission, compare detailed quotations and review the agreement before paying.
What should a house construction agreement include?
It should define scope, drawings, specifications, payments, milestones, variations, completion requirements, quality provisions, warranties, defect rectification and dispute resolution.
Is per-square-foot pricing a red flag?
Not by itself. The concern is whether the builder can explain what the rate covers, how the area is measured and which items are excluded.
What should I do if the builder asks for extra payments?
Compare the request with the agreement and approved variations. Ask for an itemised explanation and supporting documentation, and obtain independent advice if the charge is disputed.
Should I visit a builder's completed projects?
Yes, where access is available and permission is obtained. Visits and independent conversations with previous clients can reveal information that photographs alone cannot establish.
Is a verbal warranty sufficient?
A written warranty is easier to assess and document. Ask for the applicable coverage, duration, exclusions and defect-reporting process.
Can I change builders after construction has started?
It may be possible, but the consequences depend on the agreement, completed work, payments, materials and applicable law. Obtain independent advice before terminating a contract or appointing a replacement.
Conclusion Section

Conclusion

Hiring a builder involves more than comparing construction rates and viewing finished homes. A clear scope, documented material specifications, appropriate payment safeguards, competent technical supervision and defined handover responsibilities provide a stronger basis for decision-making.

Ask direct questions, verify important claims and document the agreed terms before committing. If a prospective builder repeatedly refuses to explain essential costs or provide reasonable contractual safeguards, consider postponing the engagement and obtaining independent advice.
