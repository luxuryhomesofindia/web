A package rate is meaningful only when you know what it includes. Before using a quoted rate to estimate your project, confirm:

  • The billing-area definition and measurement method.
  • The architectural and structural scope.
  • Foundation and site-condition assumptions.
  • Material specifications and fitting allowances.
  • Interior and external-work inclusions.
  • Applicable taxes, professional fees and statutory charges.
  • Optional features, exclusions and variation procedures.

The goal is not simply to find a price per square foot. It is to understand the work, materials and responsibilities covered by that price.

Package pricing versus a bespoke luxury-home budget

A construction package normally describes a defined scope of work and a specified set of materials. A bespoke home may include requirements that fall outside that specification.

Examples include large glazed openings, double-height spaces, custom joinery, upgraded finishes, home automation, swimming pools and other individually designed amenities. These features do not automatically make a package unsuitable. They make it important to identify where the package ends and project-specific pricing begins.

Budget Component What to Confirm in Writing
Main construction Structural, masonry and finishing scope.
Design and engineering Included professional services and separately priced work.
Materials and fittings Product specifications, models and allowances.
Interiors Kitchens, wardrobes, furniture and decorative lighting.
Building services Electrical, plumbing, air-conditioning and equipment scope.
External works Compound walls, gates, paving, drainage and landscaping.
Specialist amenities Lifts, pools, automation and associated equipment.
Other project costs Applicable taxes, statutory charges and excluded services.

Ask for a written inclusions-and-exclusions schedule. An “all-inclusive” label is not a substitute for a defined contract scope.

What affects the cost of building a luxury home?

Figure 2: South Indian courtyard home showcasing how site planning, natural terracotta materials, and outdoor garden spaces influence overall design and execution budgets.

1. Plot conditions and foundation requirements

Soil conditions, excavation, site access, levels and foundation design can affect the work required. Do not assume that a standard foundation allowance is appropriate for every plot. Site investigation and the structural engineer’s design should guide the solution.

2. Architectural and structural complexity

Large spans, double-height living areas, unusual roof forms, extensive glazing and custom architectural details can affect structural design, quantities and construction methods. Consider their cost implications while developing the design, rather than after the main scope has been fixed.

3. Material and finish specifications

Flooring, windows, doors, sanitary fittings, hardware and joinery vary by material, model, finish and installation requirements. A quotation should identify the applicable specification or allowance. The word “premium” alone does not establish which product is included.

4. Building services and equipment

Electrical systems, plumbing, hot-water systems, air-conditioning and automation need a clearly defined scope. Confirm whether the quotation covers equipment supply, installation, wiring, controls, commissioning and associated work where applicable.

5. Interior execution and furniture

Civil construction and interior execution are not necessarily the same scope. Check whether kitchens, wardrobes, storage, decorative lighting, curtains, loose furniture and appliances are included, excluded or offered separately.

6. External development and landscaping

Compound walls, entrance gates, driveways, drainage, outdoor lighting and landscaped areas can require separate budgets. Include them in the overall plan even when they sit outside the main building contract.

7. Specialist features

Swimming pools, lifts and advanced home-automation systems require their own design, equipment, installation and maintenance considerations. Confirm the exact scope and any specialist coordination requirements before treating these features as part of a package.

Understand allowances, not just brand names

A named brand does not establish which product or model is included. A material allowance also needs to be understood in terms of quantity, quality, wastage and installation.

For each significant item, ask:

  • What product, model or specification is covered?
  • Does the allowance include installation?
  • How are additional quantities priced?
  • Who approves substitutions if a product is unavailable?
  • How are upgrades calculated and documented?

LHI’s published package specification should be confirmed against the actual proposal for your project. Do not assume that a previous specification or online price automatically applies to a new quotation.

Built-up area, carpet area and plot area are different

Before multiplying an area by a construction rate, confirm what “sq. ft.” means in the quotation.

  • Plot area: The land area of the property.
  • Built-up area: The building area measured according to the stated measurement convention.
  • Carpet area: The usable floor area defined under the applicable measurement method.

Ask how walls, staircases, balconies, terraces, covered parking, service areas and double-height spaces are treated. Two quotations can appear different because they use different area calculations. Compare the measurement basis as well as the price.

How to plan the complete home-building budget

Start with the defined construction contract, then identify costs that sit outside it. Your budget worksheet should include:

Budget Item Action or Status
Main construction contract Obtain a written quotation.
Design and specialist services Confirm included and separate services.
Interior work Confirm scope and allowances.
External works and landscaping Obtain separate costs if excluded.
Equipment and specialist features Price the selected requirements.
Applicable taxes and statutory charges Confirm applicability and treatment.
Contingency Agree an appropriate provision based on unresolved risks.

Avoid counting an item twice. A cost already included in the main contract should not be added again as a separate expense. The contingency should reflect the maturity of the design, site information, procurement decisions and contract scope. No single percentage is appropriate for every project.

A simple way to compare construction quotations

Use the same checklist for each builder:

Comparison Point Questions to Ask
Site and foundation Are the assumptions suitable for your plot?
Materials Are product specifications and allowances comparable?
Building services Which equipment, installation and commissioning work is included?
Interiors Which rooms, fittings and furniture items are covered?
External works Are gates, paving, drainage and landscaping included?
Taxes and fees Which charges are included or excluded?
Payment schedule What work or milestone triggers each payment?
Variations How are changes priced and approved?
Handover What documentation, testing and completion items are included?

A lower initial quotation is not necessarily a lower total cost when important work is excluded. Compare equivalent scopes before making a decision.
