A published package rate is a starting reference, not a complete project quotation. The final amount depends on the agreed scope, building design, site conditions, material selections and work that falls outside the package.

Before using any per-square-foot figure for budgeting, confirm:

  • Which package and specification the rate applies to.
  • How the chargeable built-up area is measured.
  • Which materials and installation work are included.
  • Whether interiors and external works are included.
  • How upgrades and changes are priced.
  • Which taxes, fees and other charges apply.

Ask for the approved package specification and a written quotation before comparing prices.

Understand material allowances before upgrading

A material allowance establishes the budget assigned to a specified item or selection. It does not automatically cover every product, accessory or installation requirement.

Before approving an upgrade, clarify:

  • The allowance and quantity covered by the agreement.
  • Whether the allowance includes supply, installation or both.
  • The product specification and approved alternatives.
  • Any additional preparation, accessories or finishing work.
  • How the difference in cost will be calculated.
  • Whether the change affects procurement or the construction programme.

For example, selecting a different flooring material may change the purchase cost, installation method, detailing or preparation required. The actual impact depends on the selected product and project conditions. Request a written variation showing the additional cost and scope before approving the change.

What can change the final construction cost?

1. Site and foundation conditions

Soil conditions, excavation, site access and foundation design can affect the required work. Confirm which site assumptions underpin the quotation and how additional work will be handled if conditions differ.

2. Building design and configuration

Building dimensions, floor heights, structural spans, large openings, staircases, terraces and double-height spaces can change quantities and execution requirements.

3. Materials and finishes

Flooring, glazing, sanitaryware, joinery and decorative finishes vary by specification. Record the selected product or allowance instead of relying on descriptions such as “premium finish.”

4. Electrical and mechanical services

Air-conditioning, hot-water systems, home automation and specialist equipment require clear definitions of supply, installation and associated work.

5. Interior requirements

A construction package should not be assumed to include a complete interior fit-out. Check kitchens, wardrobes, storage, decorative lighting, furniture and appliances individually.

6. External development

Compound walls, gates, driveways, paving, drainage and landscaping should be listed separately unless the contract explicitly includes them.

7. Design changes and variations

Changes after approval can affect quantities, material orders, labour and sequencing. Agree on the variation approval process before work begins.

Confirm the chargeable area

A rate per square foot is meaningful only when you know how the area is measured. Ask the estimating team to define how the quotation treats:

  • Internal and external walls.
  • Staircases and circulation spaces.
  • Balconies and covered parking.
  • Service areas and terraces.
  • Double-height spaces.
  • Other covered or external structures.

Plot area, carpet area and built-up area describe different measurements. They should not be treated as interchangeable.

Plan for the complete project budget

The main construction contract may not cover every cost associated with completing and occupying a home. Organise the budget into these categories:

  • Contracted construction: work explicitly included in the signed scope.
  • Separately priced work: upgrades and items excluded from the main package.
  • Professional and statutory costs: applicable design fees, approvals, charges and taxes, according to the agreed arrangement.
  • Interior and external development: only the work not already covered by the contract.
  • Contingency: a project-specific allowance for unresolved design or site risks.

Avoid counting an item twice when it is already included in the main contract. The appropriate contingency depends on the maturity of the design, the site information and the remaining uncertainties; one percentage should not be assumed suitable for every project.

How to compare premium construction quotations

Two quotations are comparable only when their scope, assumptions and measurement methods are sufficiently aligned. Use this checklist before making a decision:

  • Compare the chargeable area and measurement method.
  • Confirm foundation and site assumptions.
  • Match structural and material specifications.
  • Compare flooring and finish allowances.
  • Check electrical, plumbing and building-services scope.
  • Identify interior and external works.
  • Separate included work from upgrades and exclusions.
  • Confirm taxes and other applicable charges.
  • Review payment milestones and variation procedures.
  • Clarify handover requirements and applicable contractual protections.

A lower initial rate does not necessarily mean a lower total commitment. Compare the complete scope and the likely cost of items outside the contract.
