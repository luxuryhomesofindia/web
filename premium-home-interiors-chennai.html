What makes home interiors feel premium?

Premium interiors are not simply a matter of choosing expensive finishes. The result depends on how layout, materials, furniture, lighting and practical details work together.

A considered interior plan can help you:

  • Arrange Furniture for Movement: Maintain comfortable circulation paths between entrance, living, dining, and outdoor zones.
  • Organise Household Storage: Customize built-in storage around your daily storage habits and accessibility needs.
  • Coordinate Kitchen Workflows: Align cabinetry layouts with high-performance appliances, plumbing lines, and electrical sockets.
  • Layer Lighting Activities: Combine task, ambient, and accent illumination for different times of day.
  • Select Resilient Materials: Choose low-maintenance, high-durability surfaces suited to humid Chennai weather.
  • Resolve Technical Interfaces: Align joinery cut-outs, ceiling duct drops, and electrical outlets before site installation.
  • Establish Visual Cohesion: Maintain a consistent material and color palette across connected living areas.
  • Integrate Smart Technology: Incorporate automation controls where they make daily routines simpler and more efficient.

The objective is a home that feels cohesive and remains practical after the initial impression has passed.

Interior design for the way you live

Living and dining areas

Living and dining spaces need to balance comfort, circulation and the way you entertain. Consider furniture dimensions, seating arrangements, lighting and connections between adjoining spaces. Where the layout allows, coordinate the dining area, living room and outdoor views without compromising comfortable movement.

Bedrooms and wardrobes

Bedrooms should support rest, privacy and organisation. Plan wardrobe interiors around the clothes and belongings you need to store. Review hanging space, drawers, shelving, door clearances and access to bedside furniture before confirming the design.

Kitchens and utility spaces

Kitchen planning should bring cabinetry, appliances, work surfaces and services into one coordinated layout. Review preparation space, storage, ventilation, cleaning needs and appliance dimensions. Confirm plumbing and electrical requirements before finalising joinery.

Bathrooms

Bathroom detailing needs to balance appearance, function and maintenance. Coordinate fittings, surfaces, lighting, drainage and waterproofing requirements. Confirm the installation scope and retain access to components that may require future maintenance.

Home offices and multipurpose rooms

A home office or flexible room should be planned around the activities it needs to support. Consider desk dimensions, equipment, lighting, privacy, acoustics and storage. If the room serves several purposes, decide how it will transition between them before selecting furniture.

Custom storage that earns its place

Storage works best when it responds to your actual routines rather than relying on a standard arrangement.

A wardrobe may need a different balance of hanging space, drawers and shelves from another household's. Kitchen storage should keep frequently used items accessible. Media furniture needs space for equipment, wiring and ventilation.

Before confirming custom furniture, review:

  • The exact inventory of items to be stored in each unit.
  • Frequency of daily access for drawers, hanging rails, and overhead racks.
  • Comfortable reach heights and soft-close mechanism operation.
  • Door swing radius and drawer clearance paths.
  • Concealed electrical outlets and LED sensor lighting wiring.
  • Access points for cleaning and future maintenance.
  • Specific materials, hardware brands, and protective topcoats included.

LHI's published offering includes custom furniture. Confirm whether design, fabrication, supply and installation are included in your specific project proposal.

Lighting planned alongside the interiors

Lighting should be coordinated with furniture, ceilings, joinery and daylight.

Consider general lighting for everyday activities, task lighting for cooking or reading, and feature lighting where it contributes to the room's function and character.

Before installation, confirm fixture positions, control requirements, wiring, access for maintenance and compatibility with any proposed automation. Resolving these details early can reduce avoidable changes to completed ceilings, cabinetry and finishes.

Smart-home integration with a clear purpose

Smart-home features should make everyday activities easier to manage. If automation is part of your brief, identify the functions you want before coordinating equipment, wiring and controls with the interior plan.

Confirm what each system will do, how residents will operate it, where equipment will be installed, how it can be accessed and who is responsible for installation and support. Choose technology based on the household's needs rather than treating the number of automated features as a measure of quality.

Planning an interior budget

Interior costs depend on the rooms involved, material selections, quantities, furniture requirements and agreed execution scope.

A useful starting point is to separate essential functional requirements from optional upgrades. Kitchen functionality, storage, lighting and furniture may have different priorities for different households.

Before approving a proposal, review:

  • Design deliverables, 3D visualisations, and revision scope.
  • Material specifications, tile allowances, and prime cost sums.
  • Joinery board quantities, veneer grades, and hardware brands.
  • Loose furniture, soft furnishings, and lighting inclusions.
  • Appliance, sanitaryware, and fixture supply roles.
  • Procurement, site transport, and installation labor scope.
  • Taxes, exclusions, and payment milestone terms.
  • Variation approval workflows and unit rate schedules.

Do not assume that a description such as “complete interiors” includes every appliance, decorative item, fixture or installation activity. The written agreement should establish the actual scope.

What to discuss before interior execution begins

  1. Establish Your Requirements: Gather available floor plans, room photographs, reference images and a list of functional priorities. Identify existing furniture or finishes you intend to retain.
  2. Review the Design Direction: Discuss layouts, material preferences, lighting, storage and how the rooms connect. Identify decisions that affect electrical, plumbing, ceiling or joinery work.
  3. Confirm Specifications & Responsibilities: Document dimensions, materials, quantities, allowances, supply responsibilities and exclusions before procurement.
  4. Coordinate the Work: Agree how interior work interfaces with architecture and building services. Establish how changes, substitutions and approvals will be managed.
  5. Define Completion Checks: Clarify installation inspections, snagging, handover information and applicable product documentation in the agreed scope.
