Does every luxury home in Chennai go directly to CMDA?

No.

The applicable authority depends on the property, proposed development and the powers applicable to the relevant local body.

Within the Chennai Metropolitan Area, some planning-permission functions are handled through delegated local-body powers, while other development categories are submitted to CMDA. The Greater Chennai Corporation also publishes specific residential categories and procedures that it processes through its Town Planning system.

Before preparing the final application, establish:

  • Jurisdiction: The property's exact location and jurisdiction.
  • Local Body: The relevant local body.
  • Category: The proposed building category.
  • Authority: The authority responsible for the application.
  • Submission Route: The current submission route.
  • Clearances: Any additional clearances that may apply.

A home's luxury specification does not, by itself, determine the approval route.

Review the plot before freezing the design

For a bespoke home, planning should begin with the site rather than the elevation.

The design team should establish the information needed to understand what can be proposed on the property, including:

  • Property Records: Property records and site identification.
  • Boundaries & Dimensions: Plot boundaries and dimensions.
  • Access Information: Access and road information.
  • Land Use: Applicable land use.
  • Existing Approvals: Existing structures and approvals.
  • Development Controls: Relevant development controls.
  • Site Restrictions: Site-specific restrictions or clearances.

The planning-permission review is not a substitute for an independent legal review of ownership documents. Where ownership, title or other legal matters require specialist advice, obtain it separately.

Bring luxury features into the planning discussion early

High-end homes often contain features that need careful coordination between architectural intent, structure, services and applicable requirements.

The safest approach is to evaluate these features before the drawings are finalised rather than adding them after the main proposal has been developed.

Building Form & Floor Arrangement

Review the proposed footprint, number of floors and overall building form against the requirements applicable to the property.

Double-Height Living Spaces

Coordinate the architectural plans, sections and structural concept. Do not assume how a particular space will be treated for approval purposes without professional review.

Balconies, Terraces & Projections

Confirm the applicable treatment before fixing these elements into the design.

Courtyards & Outdoor Areas

Consider their relationship with the building, access, drainage, open areas and the wider site-planning requirements.

Specialist Amenities

Pools, lifts, rooftop structures and other amenities can introduce additional design or approval considerations. Their requirements should be confirmed for the specific proposal.

Planning permission and building permit are not interchangeable terms

A homeowner should understand which permissions apply to the project rather than treating every approval as one generic “CMDA approval.”

CMDA publishes planning-permission information, while the Greater Chennai Corporation publishes procedures covering planning permission, where applicable, and building permits for the categories it processes.

Ask the project team to explain:

  • Required Permissions: Which permissions are required.
  • Responsible Authority: Which authority is responsible for each stage.
  • Homeowner Responsibilities: Which tasks belong to the homeowner.
  • Professional Tasks: Which tasks belong to the architect or other registered professionals.
  • Statutory Charges: Which statutory charges may apply.
  • Pre-Construction Conditions: Which conditions need to be satisfied before construction.

This distinction can prevent an approval discussion from becoming unclear later in the project.

Prepare drawings and documents for the actual proposal

There is no single document list that should be assumed for every luxury-home application.

The applicable authority and proposal determine the required submission set. Depending on the project, preparation may involve:

  • Property & Site Data: Property and site information.
  • Measurements: Survey or site measurements.
  • Architectural Set: Architectural drawings.
  • Declarations: Relevant declarations.
  • Engineering Set: Engineering information.
  • Approval History: Existing approval records.
  • NOCs & Clearances: Additional clearance documents where applicable.

Use the current authority requirements to establish the final submission set.

The Greater Chennai Corporation's published procedure also describes the role of the applicant and registered professionals in preparing and submitting planning-permission and building-permit applications for the categories it handles.

Understand professional fees and statutory charges separately

Professional fees and authority-assessed charges are different categories of cost.

Before proceeding, request a clear breakdown of:

  • Service Fees: Architectural and coordination fees.
  • Statutory Fees: Authority-assessed charges.
  • Specialist Costs: Survey or specialist-report costs.
  • Revision Fees: Additional revision costs.
  • Taxes: Applicable taxes.
  • Exclusions: Excluded expenses.

Do not rely on a generic online “CMDA approval cost” figure for a specific property.

Applicable charges can depend on the proposal and its characteristics. Obtain the current assessment relevant to the project.

Treat the programme as a process, not a guaranteed approval date

A realistic programme should distinguish between:

  • Initial Prep: Site and document preparation.
  • Design Phase: Design development.
  • Coordination: Professional coordination.
  • Submission: Application submission.
  • Review Stage: Authority review.
  • Revisions: Queries or requested revisions.
  • Compliance: Payments and compliance steps.
  • Issuance: Issuance or collection of the applicable approval documents.

The authority's review period and the homeowner's response time are not the same thing.

Avoid treating an estimated programme as a guaranteed sanction date.

Build according to the approved proposal

Planning permission is not the end of the compliance responsibility.

Once permission has been obtained, the construction should follow the approved proposal and applicable conditions.

If you want to change the layout, building form, floor arrangement or another approved element, ask the authorised professional to review the change before construction proceeds.

A change being technically or structurally possible does not automatically mean that it is permitted.
