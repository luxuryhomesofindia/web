Define the execution scope before work begins

A clear interior-contracting scope establishes what will be supplied, installed and reviewed—and what remains the responsibility of the homeowner, designer or specialist supplier.

Before approving a quotation, establish:

  • Work Packages: The specific rooms, installations, built-in joinery, and surface finishes included in the agreement.
  • Drawings & Specifications: The approved 2D working drawings, ceiling plans, and material schedules governing execution.
  • Procurement Responsibilities: Who purchases core materials, decorative lights, sanitary fittings, and hardware.
  • Joinery & Furniture: Board or timber specifications, veneer grades, internal fittings, and installation standards.
  • Trade Interfaces: Coordination of electrical, plumbing, air conditioning, and other specialist contractor works.
  • Site Protection & Conditions: Site access rules, floor protection during work, waste disposal, and working hours.
  • Variation Management: Formal procedures for approving product substitutions, extra items, and cost adjustments.
  • Completion & Handover: Agreed quality checks, operational testing, snag list rectification, and warranty documentation.

The aim is to establish responsibilities before installation—not resolve avoidable ambiguities on site.

Residential interior execution: what needs coordination?

Joinery and built-in storage

Wardrobes, storage units and built-in furniture need to follow approved dimensions and specifications. Confirm board or timber specifications, finishes, hardware, internal fittings and access to concealed services before fabrication.

Kitchens and utility areas

Kitchen cabinetry must work with countertops, appliances, plumbing, electrical points and ventilation requirements. Review appliance dimensions and service connections before finalizing fabrication details.

Precision execution detail: custom teak joinery with brushed brass handles, soft-close hardware, and flush marble counter alignment.

Flooring and surface finishes

Specify the material, layout and installation requirements. Substrate preparation, floor levels, transitions and junctions with adjoining finishes all affect the completed result.

Bathrooms

Coordinate fittings, surfaces, lighting and service connections. Clarify responsibility for waterproofing, drainage details, testing and maintenance access where applicable to the agreed scope.

Custom furniture

Bespoke furniture should suit the room, intended use and approved design. Record dimensions, materials, upholstery, hardware, delivery arrangements and installation requirements.

Smart-home interfaces

Automation provisions may affect ceilings, joinery, electrical layouts and equipment locations. Confirm who supplies, installs, configures and supports each system before work proceeds.

Specify materials, not just the finish description

Terms such as “premium” or “luxury quality” do not establish what will be supplied.

For each important item, confirm:

  • Exact product or material specification (e.g., BWP Grade Plywood, solid Teak, 20mm Italian Marble).
  • Finish, shade code, and texture.
  • Hardware brand and model codes (e.g., Blum soft-close hinges, Hafele locks).
  • Quantity or measurement basis (sq. ft., running ft., lump sum allowance).
  • Installation method and substrate preparation.
  • Approved physical sample, signed off before ordering.
  • Formal substitution and client approval procedure.
  • Manufacturer warranties and maintenance instructions.

Samples help establish appearance. Written specifications define the procurement and execution requirements.

How to evaluate an interior-contracting quotation

Compare equivalent work packages rather than headline totals.

A useful proposal should clarify:

  • Quantities and measurement assumptions.
  • Materials, specifications and allowances.
  • Fabrication and installation responsibilities.
  • Procurement responsibilities.
  • Specialist-supplier interfaces.
  • Site preparation and protection, where included.
  • Delivery and site access arrangements.
  • Taxes, exclusions and separately priced items.
  • Payment milestones linked to verified progress stages.
  • Variation procedures and change order forms.
  • Agreed completion requirements and snagging process.

Manage changes and substitutions in writing

A product change can affect dimensions, adjoining finishes, service connections, cost and delivery arrangements.

Before approving a change, document:

  • The exact technical reason for the proposed change.
  • The alternative product model or specification.
  • Any drawing, structural, or electrical implications.
  • The cost difference (addition or credit).
  • The potential impact on the project timeline.
  • Written authorization from the homeowner or authorized representative.

Define completion requirements from the start

Agree on how the installed work will be reviewed before the project begins.

Depending on the contract, completion requirements may include:

  • Installation verification against approved working drawings.
  • Smooth operation of all doors, drawers, sliding panels, and hardware mechanisms.
  • Finish quality checks for paint, polish, veneers, and silicone joints.
  • Testing of lighting circuits, plumbing fixtures, and integrated systems.
  • Formal snagging inspection report and rectification sign-off.
  • Manufacturer product manuals and maintenance instructions.
  • Warranty documentation for hardware, appliances, and waterproof coatings.
